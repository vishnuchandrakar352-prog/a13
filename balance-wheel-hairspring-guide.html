The Balance Wheel & Hairspring

Every mechanical watch has a tiny wheel swinging back and forth several times a second. It is the part that actually keeps time, and it is beautifully simple.

contents

  2. The hairspring
  3. Beats and frequency

  5. Why temperature and position matter

01.1

A wheel that swings

The balance wheel is a small, carefully weighted ring on a pivot. It does not spin round; it swings one way, then back, like a pendulum laid flat. Each swing takes the same amount of time, and the watch counts those swings to measure seconds.

01.2

The hairspring

A fine coiled spring, thinner than a hair, is fixed to the wheel’s centre.

  • As the wheel turns, the spring winds or unwinds.
  • The spring then pushes it back the other way.
  • Spring stiffness and wheel weight set the rhythm.

01.3

Beats and frequency

Each swing in one direction is a beat. A modern watch commonly beats 28,800 times per hour, which is 8 beats or 4 complete oscillations each second, or 4 hertz. Higher frequencies are steadier when the watch is knocked, but use more energy.

01.4

The escapement keeps it going

Friction would soon stop the wheel. The escapement gives it a tiny push on each swing using energy from the mainspring, and at the same moment lets the gear train advance by one step. That is the tick you hear.

01.5

Why temperature and position matter

Metal expands with heat and gravity pulls on a wheel differently when the watch is flat or upright. Watchmakers fight this with special alloys, balanced screws on the rim and careful adjustment in several positions. This is where accuracy is won or lost.
